    About the data

    Sources. Suspension figures are from the New York State Education Department (NYSED), released through a Freedom of Information Law request (FL-EM-25-1398) filed by a partner organization and shared with WCA. They cover every Westchester school district from to . Felony arrest figures are from the NYS Division of Criminal Justice Services (DCJS), shared with a youth justice coalition WCA belongs to. District outlines are from Westchester County GIS.

    How to read the numbers. "Sent home" means an out-of-school suspension; "suspended in school" means a student was taken out of class but kept in the building. A rate is the number of students suspended at least once during the school year, divided by the students enrolled on NYSED's October count day. "Times as often" is one group's rate divided by another's, or by the average. Westchester's totals include the county's special act districts and charter schools.

    Small numbers. A group's rate isn't shown when fewer than students are in it. Groups are compared only when each had at least students sent home.

    Years. School years run from September to June. Schools closed in March 2020 and taught mostly remotely in 2020-21, so those two years had far fewer suspensions.

    What the data can't show. It can't follow one child from a suspension to an arrest; it doesn't say why students were suspended; and it has no arrests or police referrals at school.
